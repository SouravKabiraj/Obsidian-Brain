I would see that as a good thing. If the team is always agreeing, then it is a bad outcome. If they are always agreeing, it means one or more of the following:

- They don’t care or are disinterested
- They don’t have a view of their own
- They are afraid to voice disagreement due to some reason
- The idea is so good that they are always in agreement

I don’t think it is humanly possible to have a 100% record of good ideas or views for anyone. I am no exception. Hence, I would welcome the disagreement. I will, in such a situation do the following:

- Acknowledge their viewpoint
- Listen with intent what and why they are disagreeing
- Try to see if they have any data/ logic/ anecdote that I am missing
- Re evaluate the idea given the new information ( take some time for it if required)
- Discuss with them again. One of three things may happen:
    - They are right- so I will change my stance
    - I still seem to be right, given all the new information and without the need of any new information- try to convince them
    - We need new information/ experiment/ research to conclusively decide- in that case get the same and repeat the process
- If still there is no closure to the discussion, seeking external/ manager help
