**Situation:** During a sprint planning session at Temenos, my senior team member suggested adding two new features to the release. However, while analyzing the backlog, I noticed that several customer-reported bugs were recurring and significantly impacting user experience. I believed fixing these bugs should take precedence over shipping new features. 

**Task:** I had to convince the team to prioritize addressing these critical bugs, even though it meant delaying new feature development. 

**Action:** 
I gathered data on customer complaints and demonstrated how these bugs were affecting the product’s reliability. 
Using the Effort, Value and Risk framework I prioritised the bugs. 
I presented this to the team and stakeholders, showing that delaying feature development to resolve these issues would lead to better long-term product stability and customer satisfaction. 
Though some were hesitant about postponing feature delivery, I stood firm and ensured that the decision was in the best interest of our users. 

**Result:** Ultimately, the team agreed to focus on bug fixes first, pushing new features to a later sprint. This decision led to increased customer satisfaction, fewer escalations, and a stronger, more reliable product.
